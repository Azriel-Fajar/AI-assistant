Build a 14-slide presentation deck about **GCLS+ (Geneva College of Longevity Science) AI in Healthcare Certification** — https://gcls.academy/

## Audience & Purpose

Presenting to a **university rector, deans, heads of faculty, and practicing physicians** at an Indonesian university. This is an **informational and benefits** briefing, not a sales pitch. The audience is senior, academically credentialed, and skeptical of hype. They want to understand: what this program is, whether it is credible, and what their institution and their clinicians would actually gain from it.

Tone: authoritative, evidence-led, plain-spoken. No marketing superlatives. No exclamation marks. Let the numbers and the credentials carry the persuasion. Assume the audience is intelligent but not technical — explain AI concepts in one clear sentence each, never in jargon.

Language: **English** throughout.

## Design Direction

**Futuristic, illustrated, immediately understandable.** Specifically:

- **Palette:** deep navy / near-black base (#0A0E1A to #111827), with a violet-to-cyan accent gradient (#7C3AED → #06B6D4). One warm accent (amber #F59E0B) reserved only for highlighted stats. Generous negative space.
- **Typography:** a geometric sans for headings (Space Grotesk, Outfit, or similar), a clean neutral sans for body (Inter). Large, confident headline sizes. Never more than ~35 words of body copy per slide.
- **Illustration style:** custom vector/SVG illustrations and diagrams — abstract medical-tech motifs (neural node networks, stethoscope-meets-circuit, layered data planes, molecular lattices, clinical workflow paths). Line-art with gradient strokes and soft glow, not flat corporate clipart and **not** stock photography of doctors pointing at tablets.
- **Futuristic cues, used with restraint:** subtle grid overlays, thin glowing dividers, glassmorphic cards with low-opacity fills and 1px luminous borders, soft radial glows behind key numbers.
- **Every content slide must carry a visual** — a diagram, an icon system, a stat block, or an illustration. Never a slide that is only a bulleted list.
- **Readability is the hard constraint.** High contrast, no text over busy imagery, minimum 20px body text. This is projected in a lecture hall, potentially a bright one.
- Consistent slide furniture: a thin progress indicator, section label top-left, slide number bottom-right.

## Slide-by-Slide Structure

### Slide 1 — Title
GCLS+ AI in Healthcare Certification. Subtitle: "Build the Judgment to Use, Govern, and Lead Clinical AI." Line: "Geneva College of Longevity Science." Full-bleed hero illustration: abstract neural-network-meets-anatomy motif.

### Slide 2 — The Context
Why this matters now. Clinicians and health-system leaders are already being asked to make AI decisions — whether or not they feel ready to. The question has shifted from "will AI enter healthcare" to "who in this institution is qualified to judge it."
Visual: a simple timeline or before/after split illustration.

### Slide 3 — What GCLS+ Is
A clinician-first, **non-degree professional education program**. Built for physicians, clinical teams, practice leaders, and healthcare executives.
Be explicit and honest about the boundary — it earns credibility with this audience: *the program does not grant academic credit, professional licensure, or a regulated clinical specialty credential.* Learners who complete the required coursework and capstone earn the **GCLS+ Certificate in AI in Healthcare**.
Visual: a clean "what it is / what it is not" two-column card layout.

### Slide 4 — Program at a Glance
Four hero stats in glassmorphic stat cards:
- **11 modules** + capstone
- **100+ hours** of structured learning
- **4 pillars** of competency
- **Self-paced**, with weekly live support
Visual: large gradient numerals with soft glow.

### Slide 5 — The Four Pillars
Present as four illustrated quadrants. Every module maps to one or more:
1. **AI Fluency & Technical Foundations** — how LLMs, prompt engineering, agentic AI, MCP servers, and systems architecture actually work.
2. **Clinical Application** — cognitive blueprints, clinical decision support validation, longevity medicine, safety frameworks.
3. **Operations & Strategy** — workflow automation, revenue cycle optimization, change management, deployment where it drives real ROI.
4. **Governance, Law & Ethics** — SaMD classification, data protection, HIPAA, jurisdiction-specific AI regulation.

### Slide 6 — Curriculum, Modules 1–5
Present as a visual pathway/track, not a table. One short descriptor each:
- **Module 1 — The AI-Native Mindset:** AI-as-teammate, cognitive load management, the 10x test, workflow mapping.
- **Module 2 — AI Fluency Foundations:** how LLMs work, the tool landscape, NotebookLM, token economics.
- **Module 3 — AI in Healthcare Today:** regulatory landscape by jurisdiction, SaMD classification, data protection, ethics.
- **Module 4 — Prompt Engineering:** clinical prompt patterns, chain-of-thought reasoning, template libraries, prompt security.
- **Module 5 — Your Cognitive Blueprint:** self-assessment, decision-making mapping, personal AI interaction style.

### Slide 7 — Curriculum, Modules 6–10 + Capstone
Same visual pathway, continued:
- **Module 6 — AI-Powered Workflows:** documentation automation, RCM and billing optimization, organizational change management.
- **Module 7 — Agentic AI Building:** build-vs-buy frameworks, automation platforms, agentic workflow costs, AI ROI.
- **Module 8 — AI-Native Systems Design:** multi-agent patterns, orchestration, architecture, EHR integration.
- **Module 9 — CDS & Validation:** checking for bias, human oversight, catching errors before they reach patients, adverse event reporting.
- **Module 10 — AI in Longevity Medicine:** multi-omics, digital twins, biological age assessment, in-silico trials, precision prevention.
- **Capstone:** build, deploy, and implement a real AI-powered healthcare tool.

### Slide 8 — The Learning Arc
Four stages, shown as an illustrated progression from left to right:
1. **Fluency** — how AI systems work, where they fail, how data, prompts, models, and agents shape the result.
2. **Application** — AI in research, patient communication, documentation, scheduling, operations, with safety guardrails in place.
3. **Judgment** — evaluate vendor claims, workflow fit, validation, privacy, liability, and total cost; build, buy, or skip with confidence.
4. **Implementation** — turn one real organizational problem into a capstone dossier with scoped workflow, governance assumptions, success metrics, monitoring, and a pilot plan.

- AI-assisted documentation, differential support, patient communication
- Financial dashboards, staffing models, revenue cycle optimization
- Appointment optimization, no-show prediction, capacity planning
- HIPAA-safe prompting, PHI handling, audit-ready documentation
Framing line: every module is designed around tasks physicians and clinic leaders actually do.

## Rules

- Do not invent statistics, accreditation claims, CME credits, university affiliations, or student numbers beyond what is written above.
- Preserve the honest limitations exactly as stated — the non-degree boundary on Slide 3 and the no-CME note on Slide 13. This audience will check, and the candour is what earns their trust.
- Keep every slide readable from the back of a lecture hall.
- Maintain one consistent illustration style across all 14 slides.
