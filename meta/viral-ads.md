# How to Make VIRAL Ads That Make You Millions

**Source:** [YouTube - Sabri Suby / King Kong](https://youtu.be/plB3mEMLGag?si=q9GOBjB5E-F5hVcn)
**Framework:** 8-Step Process for High-ROAS Ads

---

## Core Premise

- 80% of ad performance lives in the creative -- not the ads manager
- Creative starts with understanding the prospect, not configuring templates
- Most marketers waste time pressing buttons; pros obsess over copy and research

---

## Step 1: Research (The Foundation)

**Goal:** Get inside the brain of your dream prospect. Find the "bruised knee."

**Tool: Halo Strategy** -- a worksheet tracking what the market wants, what they're buying, what they're saying. Lets you shift copy when the market shifts.

**Research Sources:**
- **Reddit** -- find long, emotionally-charged comments. Copy exact language, don't alter it.
- **Facebook Groups** -- 80k-1M members posting daily. Watch for: complaints about existing solutions, fears, desired outcomes.
- **"Discussions" Chrome extension** -- Google search filtered to forums only. Treasure chest of raw customer language.
- **Customer service emails** -- export all, paste into ChatGPT, ask: "Top 5 common pains and benefits?"

**Key insight:** Don't address every pain. Find the ONE root problem -- the "bullseye." All other pains are symptoms. Wrap the entire ad around that one thing.

> "When you hit the bullseye, it's what causes an ad to pull its freaking head off."

---

## Step 2: Attention Hijack (Pattern Interrupt + Pattern Match)

**Goal:** Stop the scroll in a chaotic newsfeed.

### Pattern Interrupt
- Do the opposite of what everyone else is doing
- Combine **visual** + **auditory** interrupt simultaneously
- Examples: live chicken in the office, red Versace bathrobe, being zipped into a body bag
- Rule: If competitors won't do it, you should

### Pattern Match
- Use visuals your prospect sees daily -- triggers identity recognition
- Examples: Shopify dashboard for e-commerce owners, Facebook Ads Manager for media buyers
- Signals: "This is relevant to me" before a word is read

### Swipe File System
- Keep two swipe files:
  1. Competitor ads + claims
  2. Anything that hijacks YOUR attention (screenshots, interfaces, visuals)
- Document softwares, websites, UI patterns your market sees daily
- Use these as pattern match OR as context to build a pattern interrupt against

---

## Step 3: Multiple Hooks (Anti-Fatigue)

**Goal:** Keep one ad running for 1-2 years without creative fatigue.

- Shoot **10-12 hooks per ad** in one production week
- Launch with 1-2 hooks
- When those fatigue (people skip after 15s thinking they've seen it), roll out next hook
- Same body -- new opening. Audience thinks it's a new ad.

> "One week of production, I'll be running this ad for a year, maybe even 2 years."

---

## Step 4: Sustained Pattern Interrupts Throughout

**Goal:** Re-hook viewers who mentally check out mid-ad.

- Pattern interrupt doesn't end at the hook
- Viewers drift into "zombie mode" mid-ad and tune out
- Hit them with new interrupts throughout the entire ad
- Pattern after pattern after pattern -- keep re-engaging

---

## Step 5: Sell Against Alternatives (Not Your Product)

**Goal:** Overcome the prospect's prior failures and skepticism.

- Most prospects have already tried other solutions -- and failed
- If you ignore that and just pitch your product, you lose them
- **Frame:** Name and acknowledge every alternative they've tried. Show why each failed. Then show how you're different.
- Only 3% are ready to buy right now. The other 97% are skeptical from past failures. Speak to them.

> "You have to name it and frame it and then show them how you are different -- or they'll just assume it's more of the same."

**Competitor swipe file use case:** Study competitor claims. Your prospect has heard those claims and is still stuck. Use that directly in copy.

---

## Step 6: Speak Directly to Pain Points

**Goal:** Make the prospect feel understood, not sold to.

- The $34M ad worked because it spoke directly to a tired, skeptical market
- Prospect pain: agencies promised results and didn't deliver
- Copy mirrored that exact frustration back at them
- Use the exact words from research -- not marketing-speak, not hyperbole

---

## The Bullseye Principle

Every market has one root problem. All other pains are symptoms. Find it. Wrap everything around it.

- Use research to surface 5-10 pains
- Look for the common thread
- Name it once, clearly, and go deep -- not broad
